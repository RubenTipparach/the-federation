Detail

Two layers over the plates. Painted: aztec sub plates in a third tone, panel lines with a lit rivet, lifeboat rows, hatches, thruster quads, phaser strips, sensor domes, docking ports, scorch streaks and hull ticks, each one to five texels. Mounted: one kit of small meshes, domes, drums, hoops, fins, spikes, cannons and blocks, parented to the part they sit on. The Greebles button hides the mounted layer so the silhouette can be judged on its own, and the 60 pixel tile shows what survives.

Windows are decals pasted onto the hull surface, batched one mesh per part: a strip of six variants with panes lit, dim or dark, picked per window by a hash. One kind per faction, Terran panes, Kthaari slits, Vaelith ovals, Sarn hexes, Helion portholes, Bloom pores, and one size on every class, so the window is the scale bar: a dreadnought has more rows of the same pane.

Texels

Every face is projected flat along its own axis at 24 texels per unit, so a plate is the same size on a pod, a wing and a hull, and a taper foreshortens by a few percent instead of stretching. Top down faces carry a fitted picture instead: the disc top is painted once, bow up, like a sprite.

The 60 pixel tile is the game's test: a class must be identifiable from directly above at about that size. If two ships only differ in a side view, one of them is not doing its job.
